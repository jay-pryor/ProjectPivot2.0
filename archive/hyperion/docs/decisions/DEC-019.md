# DEC-019: A tempo that lands on a day the month does not have clamps to that month's last day, and the day of the month does not recover
Date: 2026-09-16
Status: accepted
Tier: interface

## Context
G3 decided SL-06's tempo unit: "a whole number of calendar months, with the due date the
same day of the month, so 'every 12 months' reads as it is meant". That rule is well defined
for every due date on days 1 to 28 and undefined for the rest: 31 January plus one month, 31
March plus one month, 29 February plus twelve. The `review-schedule` contract cannot state
C-008 — what `completeReview` sets the next due date to — without settling it, and criterion
2 is the clause a conformance test is written from, so leaving it to the implementation would
put the answer in `src/` where no contract reader or reviewer would find it.

It is not a free choice between two readings of the criterion. Overflowing into the next
month (31 January plus one month is 3 March) abandons "the same day of the month" from the
first short month onward and moves every later due date further from the day the user set, so
it contradicts the decision it would be implementing. What remains to decide is how the
clamped case behaves afterwards, and that is a real choice between two mechanisms.

## Decision
Adding a tempo of `n` months to a `DateAest` gives the date `n` months later with the same
day of the month, except that when the target month has fewer days than that, it gives that
month's last day. So 31 January plus one month is 28 February, or 29 February in a leap year;
31 March plus one month is 30 April; 29 February plus twelve months is 28 February.

The day of the month does not recover. Because `completeReview` advances the stored
`nextDueAest` and holds no other anchor (C-006, C-008), a schedule clamped to 28 February
advances to 28 March, not 31 March: the day of the month can only move earlier, once, per
clamping, and then stays. A tempo of 12 months never clamps for any due date except 29
February, so the common case is unaffected.

## Alternatives considered
### Overflow into the following month
Rejected because: it contradicts the G3 decision it would implement, as above. It also makes
a monthly schedule drift forward without bound — 31 January, 3 March, 3 April — so the tempo
a user reads as "every month" is not the tempo they get.

### A stored anchor day alongside the due date, so the day of the month recovers
The schedule would carry the day of the month the user originally set, and each advance would
apply the tempo to the anchor rather than to the stored due date: 31 January, 28 February, 31
March. Rejected because: it is a second field holding a fact the due date already carries in
every case but the clamped one, and two fields that must agree are a state that can disagree —
the malformed-record rules (C-004) would have to cover an anchor that contradicts its due
date, and every `setSchedule` would have to decide whether the user is setting the anchor or
the due date. The recovery it buys applies only to schedules whose tempo lands on a short
month, which is a tempo of 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, or a non-annual multiple, for a due date on day 29
to 31. That is a narrow gain for a permanent second source of truth (P3).

Recorded rather than dismissed, because the ratchet the chosen option accepts is the thing a
specification review is most likely to raise: the contract's not-promised section names it so
the reviewer can weigh it against this record instead of rediscovering it.

## Consequences
- Makes easy: C-008 as one sentence a conformance test is written from; a schedule that is one
  record with no field that can contradict another; month arithmetic that is a pure function of
  a date and a whole number.
- Makes hard: a user who wants a review on the last day of every month, which they get, and a
  user who wants it on the 31st of every month that has one, which they do not.
- Locks in: the due date as the only anchor a schedule holds.

## Reversal
Cost to undo: hours while no folder holds a schedule, because it is one function and the
clause that cites it. Days once schedules are stored and an anchor field has to be added to
records that do not carry one, under the rule that adding a field to a kind is an Interface
change to its owning module (DEC-005) — and the added field would have no true value for a
schedule already clamped, so the recovery would start from the clamped day anyway.
Would be triggered by: a user reporting that a monthly or quarterly review has walked earlier
in the month over a year of use, which is the only way the ratchet is felt.
