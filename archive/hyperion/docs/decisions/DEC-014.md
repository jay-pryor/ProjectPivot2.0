# DEC-014: A control reaches a platform only by a confirmation and leaves it only with a justification, each one act on the hazard-control-platform triple
Date: 2026-09-15
Status: accepted
Tier: interface

## Context
SL-04 is the slice for the two severity-1 hazards behind the control model: HZ-001, a
platform shown as having a control it never inherited, and HZ-007, a control assigned to a
platform no person confirmed it for. Its four criteria turn on one question `registry` 2.0
left open: what is stored when a hazard's control is *not* on a platform, and what is stored
when it is.

2.0 has one answer for "on": a live `control-platform` link over the triple (DEC-013). It has
no answer for "not on". `listPlatformHazards` returns `linkedToPlatform: false` for every
control without a link, which conflates two states SL-04 must tell apart — a control a person
decided against, with a reason (criteria 2 and 3), and a control nobody has ruled on yet
(criterion 4). A boolean cannot carry either the reason or the decision, and the slice cannot
be built until this is settled: the conformance suite, `views` 4.0, and the SL-07 onboarding
workflow all read whatever this decides.

## Decision
For each (hazard, control, platform) triple, `registry` 3.0 stores at most one live
`control-platform` link and at most one live `Justification`, and never both. The three
states that follow are exhaustive and are what every list reports:

- **confirmed** — a live link. Its `createdBy` and `createdAtAest` are who confirmed the
  control for that platform and when (REQ-070); no second field records it.
- **excluded** — a live justification, a `justification` record over the same three ids
  carrying the text a person wrote for leaving the control off this platform (REQ-058).
- **awaiting** — neither. The state a control is in from the moment its hazard is linked to
  the platform until a person rules on it (REQ-013).

Two operations move between them, each one human act, each naming one control:
`confirmControlForPlatform` creates the link and marks any live justification for that triple
deleted; `excludeControlFromPlatform` stores the justification and marks any live link for
that triple deleted. They replace 2.0's `linkControlToPlatform` and
`unlinkControlFromPlatform`. There is no other way a `control-platform` link comes into
being: nothing creates one as a side effect, and no operation takes more than one control, so
a platform never inherits a control in bulk (HZ-007). Rows are never removed, so SL-05 can
show every confirmation and every exclusion.

## Alternatives considered
### A link row created unconfirmed, carrying a `confirmed` flag or a pending status
Rejected because: it makes a stored `control-platform` link mean "linked" sometimes and "not
linked yet" other times, so REQ-001 — a control shown as linked only when a stored link
exists — becomes true only with a qualifier every consumer must remember, and a consumer that
forgets it shows an unconfirmed control as the platform's, which is HZ-007 exactly. The
failure is in the unsafe direction, which is the one a default must never take.

### `confirmedBy` and `confirmedAtAest` as fields of the `control-platform` link
Rejected because: the record header already carries who created the row and when, and this
decision makes creating the row the confirmation, so the fields would hold a second copy of a
fact that is already stored (P3). Two fields that must agree are how they come to disagree.
An exclusion restamps only `updatedBy` and `updatedAtAest`, so the creation stamp survives as
the record of the confirmation even after the control is removed.

### The justification as a field of the link row it deletes
Rejected because: a control that was never confirmed has no link row to hang it on, and that
is the common case — criterion 2's "not linked to the platform" covers controls that never
were. It would also make a justification invisible to REQ-045's change log and REQ-011's
cross-platform warning, both of which name a justification as a record in its own right.

### A justification recorded by its own operation, with the requirement enforced at save
Rejected because: it leaves reachable a stored state REQ-058 forbids — a control decided
against with no reason stored — and puts the guard in the module that draws rather than the
one that stores (P2). `store` C-002 decides what a save needs and would have to learn the
control model to decide this one. Carrying the text into the act that makes the exclusion
means the forbidden state cannot be written at all.

### Keeping the names `linkControlToPlatform` and `unlinkControlFromPlatform`
Rejected because: the call site is the part of a contract a consumer cannot avoid reading,
and a name that says "link" invites the bulk link during onboarding (SL-07) that HZ-007 is
about. `confirmControlForPlatform` cannot be called without a person having confirmed
something, or at least without the author noticing they are claiming one.

## Consequences
- Makes easy: the three states from the one query every platform list already goes through
  (C-019); an onboarding workflow that presents the awaiting controls and nothing else; a
  change log of confirmations and exclusions, because every row stays; a reason kept per
  hazard per platform, so the same control excluded from two platforms carries two reasons.
- Makes hard: confirming many controls in one call, which SL-07 must do as a sequence of
  acts; removing a control from a platform without writing a reason; asking whether a control
  is on a platform without naming a hazard, which DEC-013 already made the wrong question.
- Locks in: the triple as the unit of confirmation and of justification;
  `collections.justification` keyed by a `JustificationId`; a confirmation and an exclusion
  each superseding the other rather than stacking.

## Reversal
Cost to undo: hours for the contract and its suite, days once data exists — every stored
justification would have to be moved or dropped, and the confirmation stamp is the header,
which cannot be recovered if a later shape stops keeping deleted rows. `views` 3.0 C-024
is the one consumer and is rewritten by `views`' SL-04 CONTRACT session, before either
SL-03 or SL-04 is implemented; SL-03's unlink becomes this exclusion, which is why that
session comes before SL-03's IMPLEMENT.
Would be triggered by: the organisation treating a control as applying to a platform by
default unless excluded, which would invert the awaiting state and make confirmation an
audit field rather than the thing that creates the link.
