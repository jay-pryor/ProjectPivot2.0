# DEC-026: A completed review sets the last reviewed date of the platform and each hazard a person decided about, and a workflow act reaches the platforms of its subject
Date: 2026-09-16
Status: accepted
Tier: interface

## Context
Two fields that no contract can default had to be settled before `workflows` 1.0 could be
written, and both are the same shape of question DEC-020 and DEC-024 answered for
`review-schedule` and for `views`: a module that does not hold a link has to be told which
platforms an act reaches, and the module that tells it has to have a rule.

First, `review-schedule.completeReview` takes a `ScheduleAct` carrying `affectedPlatformIds`,
which that contract stores as given and never derives (review-schedule C-012, DEC-020). SL-06's
contract named `workflows` as the one caller of `completeReview` in the built application, and
left the list to it.

Second, the workflow record is itself a stored record whose every change carries a change-log
entry (DEC-025, C-010), and `change-log.recordChange` requires `affectedPlatformIds` too
(`change-log` C-007, DEC-015). `registry.platformsAffected` rejects a `ref` of a kind registry
does not own with `UnownedRecordKindError` (registry C-024), and `workflow-record` is such a kind,
so the list cannot simply be asked for.

Behind both sits a third question SL-07 criterion 3 leaves open: "completing it sets the last
reviewed date of each record reviewed". Which records those are decides how many
`completeReview` calls a completion makes and therefore how many lists have to be derived.

## Decision
**The records a completed review workflow sets a last reviewed date on are the platform the
review is on and each hazard the reviewer marked reviewed at `review-the-hazards`** — the records
a person entered a decision about. For each of them, in ascending order with the platform's own
ref first, `review-schedule.getSchedule` is asked, and `completeReview` is called only where it
gives a live schedule: a record with no schedule has no date to set, is still reviewed, and still
carries its decision entry. The controls shown under a hazard's row are reviewed as part of that
row and are not called for, although `SCHEDULABLE_KINDS` would allow it.

**The `affectedPlatformIds` of each `completeReview` is `registry.platformsAffected` of that
record's own ref**, computed by `workflows`, which imports the contract that holds the links. It
is not the caller's: a screen has no reason to know which platforms a hazard reaches, and asking
the caller would put the derivation two modules away from the links it is derived from.

**The `affectedPlatformIds` of an act on the workflow record itself is
`registry.platformsAffected` of the record's `subject`** when the subject is not null and of a
kind registry owns, and empty otherwise. A workflow on a platform reaches that platform; a
workflow on a hazard reaches every platform that hazard is linked to; a workflow whose subject is
not yet created — `add-data` and `onboard-platform` before their first step — reaches nothing.
`madeForPlatformId` is `act.madeForPlatformId` unchanged, as it is everywhere, so a workflow run
on the platform the user is working on fills nobody's acknowledgement queue with its own steps
(`change-log` C-010).

## Alternatives considered
### Set a last reviewed date on the platform alone
Rejected because: criterion 3 says "each record reviewed" and a review workflow's whole substance
is going through the platform's hazards one by one. A review that touched twenty hazards and left
all twenty reading as never reviewed would make the overdue flag say the opposite of what
happened, which is HZ-009's shape.

### Set one on every control shown under a reviewed hazard as well
Rejected because: nobody entered a decision about the control. A control appears in a hazard's row
with its state and its reason (registry C-019, C-021), and the reviewer's decision is about the
hazard; setting a date from a decision that was never made is a last reviewed date that cannot be
defended when someone asks who reviewed it. The rejected reading is recorded in the contract's
preamble, and C-006's list of refs is the one clause that would change if it is the intended one.

### Set one on every hazard the query returned, decided or not
Rejected because: it makes `review-the-hazards`' demand pointless. The step exists so that each
hazard is looked at, and `stepDemand` refuses to advance while any row is undecided (C-008); if
completion set a date on every row regardless, the gate would be decoration and a review could be
completed by advancing past hazards nobody read.

### Call `completeReview` for every reviewed record and let it reject where there is no schedule
Rejected because: `UnknownScheduleError` is the right refusal for a caller that thinks a schedule
exists, and the wrong one for a completion that is asking about every record it reviewed. Under
C-009 a completion is all of its acts or none, so one hazard without a tempo would make the whole
review uncompletable — and most hazards will have no tempo, because a tempo is set per record by
a person (review-schedule C-005). Asking `getSchedule` first is one read per record and turns a
blocking rejection into an absence.

### Take `affectedPlatformIds` from the caller, as `review-schedule` does
Rejected because: `review-schedule` takes it from the caller because it imports nothing that could
derive it (DEC-020); `workflows` imports `registry`, so the argument does not carry across. Asking
the screen for a list it would have to compute from a contract this module already holds is the
second derivation DEC-016 and DEC-017 both refused, and the two could disagree.

### Give the workflow record an empty `affectedPlatformIds` always
Rejected because: a workflow run on a platform is something that platform's owner should be able
to see in the history of their platform, and an empty list is this module claiming the act reaches
nowhere when it holds the contract that says otherwise — the same objection DEC-024 raised against
an empty list for a reference entry. In practice the list is usually the one platform the act was
also made for, so the entry lands in nobody's queue anyway; the difference shows when a workflow
is run on a hazard that sits on several platforms.

### Widen `registry.platformsAffected` to answer for a `workflow-record`
Rejected because: registry holds no link to a workflow record and its refusal of an unowned kind
is deliberate (registry C-024). The subject is the thing that has links, and asking about the
subject is asking registry a question it can answer.

## Consequences
- Makes easy: a completed review whose effect on the schedule matches what the reviewer actually
  decided; one rule for both fields, derived from the links by the module that holds the
  contract; a review of a platform whose hazards mostly have no tempo, which completes and sets
  dates only where there are schedules.
- Makes hard: reviewing a control on its own — there is no workflow for it at 1.0, and
  `SCHEDULABLE_KINDS` allowing a control a tempo means a control can be overdue with no workflow
  that clears it. That gap is recorded in the contract's section 7 rather than closed by setting
  a date nobody entered.
- Locks in: `registry.platformsAffected` as the only source of an affected platform in this
  module, as it is in `views` (DEC-017, DEC-024); a completion that reads one `getSchedule` and
  one `platformsAffected` per record reviewed, which section 6 carries as its cost.

## Reversal
Cost to undo: hours. Each half is one clause — C-006's list of refs and C-010's derivation — and
nothing is stored differently: `affectedPlatformIds` lives on a `change-log` entry whose shape is
DEC-015's and does not change, and a last reviewed date is a field `review-schedule` already has.
Reversing after reviews have been completed leaves past schedules carrying dates set under this
rule and later ones under whatever replaces it, which is history and not a migration.
Would be triggered by: a ruling that a control is reviewed in its own right, which would add it to
C-006's list and probably a workflow of its own; or a workflow kind whose subject is of a record
kind `registry` does not own — a report at SL-09 — which would need the same carve-out DEC-024 made
for a `report` ref and is the moment to revisit the derivation.
