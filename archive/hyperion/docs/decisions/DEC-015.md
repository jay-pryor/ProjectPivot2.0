# DEC-015: A change-log entry is one person's act, holding every record that act changed and the platforms the caller names; an acknowledgement is an entry of its own
Date: 2026-09-16
Status: accepted
Tier: interface

## Context
SL-05 is the slice for the two severity-1 hazards behind the visible history: HZ-005, a
hazard lost or deleted with no record a user can see, and HZ-006, data of a hazard, control,
or justification changed through an edit made for another platform without that platform's
owner seeing it. DEC-004 placed `change-log` below `registry` and keyed it by platform, and
DEC-005 made the record header — who created and last changed a record and when — baseline
precisely so the change log can read it for every kind. What neither decided was the shape of
an entry, and `change-log`'s CONTRACT session cannot be written without it: three of SL-05's
six criteria (1, 2, 4) and REQ-045's "previous and new values of each changed field" all read
whatever this decides, as do the `registry` and `views` CONTRACT sessions that follow.

Two questions force it now. First, what one entry covers: one record, or one act. An
exclusion writes a justification and supersedes a `control-platform` link in a single call
(DEC-014, registry C-021), so one human act already changes two records, and REQ-012 asks for
"the change, and every item it affects" to be shown as one thing. Second, where an
acknowledgement lives: in the entry it acknowledges, or in an entry of its own.

## Decision
One `change-log-entry` record per **act** — one call, one profile, one instant. It carries
`items`, one per record the act changed, each holding that record's `RecordRef`, the action
derived from its status transition (`created`, `edited`, `deleted`, `retired`), and the
previous and new value of every field that differs, compared deep. A deletion's item carries
every field the record held, which is what REQ-010 asks be kept. The entry also carries
`madeForPlatformId`, the platform the act was made for or null, and `affectedPlatformIds`,
every platform the act reaches. Both come from the caller: the module that owns the record
knows which platforms its links reach, and `change-log` cannot ask `registry` without
creating the cycle DEC-004 rejected.

An acknowledgement is a second `change-log-entry`, told from the first by `entryKind`
(`'record-change'` or `'acknowledgement'`) as a `Link` is told by `linkKind` (DEC-013). It
names the entry acknowledged and the one platform it is acknowledged for. So every row of
this collection is written once and never altered, and what a platform still awaits is read
by subtracting the acknowledgements from the entries whose `affectedPlatformIds` hold it and
whose `madeForPlatformId` is not it.

## Alternatives considered
### One entry per record changed, rather than per act
Rejected because: an exclusion changes two records in one call (registry C-021), so a user
would acknowledge the justification and the superseded link separately, and could acknowledge
one and not the other — a state REQ-012 has no reading for. There would also be no record
holding "the change, and every item it affects", so `views` would have to reassemble the act
from the entries' timestamps and author, which is a join on two fields that are not keys.

### The acknowledgement as a field of the entry it acknowledges
Rejected because: it makes the log's rows mutable, so the same operation that writes the
record HZ-005 depends on can rewrite it, and a defect in the acknowledgement path can erase
history rather than merely fail to clear a queue. It also makes the field a map whose shape
grows with the platforms an act reached, which every later read must interpret, where an
appended entry is the shape already defined.

### A record kind of its own for acknowledgements
Rejected because: `RECORD_KINDS` is baseline (DEC-005), so adding one is a baseline change
under CORE-CHG-002 — the heaviest gate — for something the kind already reserved for this
module carries. `Link` already holds three shapes under one kind (DEC-013); this is the same
pattern at one collection lower.

### `change-log` deriving the affected platforms itself
Rejected because: the platforms a hazard, control, or justification reaches are the live
links, which are `registry`'s, and a `change-log` that imports `registry` closes the cycle
DEC-004 named — `registry` records its own changes through `change-log`. Taking the ids from
the caller keeps the graph acyclic and makes the entry a statement of what the caller
believed it was changing, which is what a reviewer needs to see.

### Deriving the whole log by diffing two working bodies at save
Rejected because: a body-to-body diff cannot say who made which change or when, only who
saved and when, and REQ-045 asks for the time and the author of the change. A record created
and deleted between two saves would leave no entry at all, which is HZ-005 exactly. It would
also have no act boundary to acknowledge, so REQ-012's queue would have nothing to key on.

### Letting the caller name the action (`created`, `deleted`, `retired`, `edited`)
Rejected because: a caller that mislabels a deletion as an edit produces a history that reads
as complete and is not, and no test of the caller can see it. The status transition already
says which it is, so `change-log` derives it and a caller cannot get it wrong.

## Consequences
- Makes easy: one entry to show and one to acknowledge per human act; a history of any record
  of any kind, including kinds not yet built, because the diff is over the header and the
  fields and knows no kind; a queue that survives a change of a platform's owner with no data
  of its own (REQ-081); an append-only collection, so a later read of old entries needs no
  migration.
- Makes hard: recording a change without knowing which platforms it reaches — every caller
  must compute that before it calls; acknowledging part of an act; correcting an entry
  written with the wrong `affectedPlatformIds`, which can only be answered by another entry.
- Locks in: the act as the unit of history and of acknowledgement; `affectedPlatformIds` as
  the caller's statement, not the log's derivation; every row of
  `collections['change-log-entry']` written once and never altered.

## Reversal
Cost to undo: hours for the contract and its suite, days once data exists — entries would
have to be split or merged, and an acknowledgement moved into the entry it names could not be
recovered for acts already acknowledged. `registry` and `views` are the two consumers and are
written against this at their own SL-05 CONTRACT sessions, before SL-05 is implemented.
Would be triggered by: an act that changes so many records that one entry is impractical to
show or to store, or a requirement that a platform's owner acknowledge part of an act; or a
second module needing to know which platforms an act reached without the caller saying so,
which would force the `registry` edge DEC-004 rejected.
