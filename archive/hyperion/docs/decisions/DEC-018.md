# DEC-018: The review schedule is its own record kind, not a field on the records it schedules
Date: 2026-09-16
Status: accepted
Tier: baseline

## Context
SL-06 puts a review tempo, a due date, and a last-reviewed date on each hazard, control,
platform, and reference entry (criteria 1 to 4), behind the `review-schedule` module
DEC-004 created for exactly that. Those records are owned by two other modules:
`registry` owns hazard, control, and platform, and `reference-register` owns the reference
entry. So the schedule data has to live somewhere, and the choice is between a record kind
of its own and three or four new fields on kinds that already exist.

`RecordKind` and `RECORD_KINDS` in `baseline/types.js` are baseline, because the change log
names every kind (REQ-045) and every module reads the list. The CONTRACT session that
writes `review-schedule` 1.0 cannot name a kind that baseline does not list, and may not
add one — `baseline/**` is a BASELINE session's to change (CORE-CHG-002). This decision is
therefore taken before that contract rather than inside it, and it is what that session's
declaration will build on.

Deferring it would leave the SL-06 CONTRACT session with no kind to own, and the only way
through would be the rejected alternative below, chosen by default rather than on its
merits.

## Decision
`review-schedule` owns its own record kind, `review-schedule`, per DEC-004's
decomposition: one record module, one kind of record. Baseline gains the kind in the
`RecordKind` typedef and in `RECORD_KINDS`, and a `ReviewScheduleId` branded typedef with
a `reviewScheduleId` identifier made by `uuidKind('review-schedule')` — a random UUID, as
for every kind but hazard (DEC-005).

Nothing else in baseline changes. `ReviewTempoMonths` and `DateAest` already exist and are
untouched. The envelope, the record header, and `DataBody` are untouched, so
`SCHEMA_VERSION` stays 1: `DataBody.collections` is a
`Partial<Record<RecordKind, …>>` (`baseline/schema.js`), so the new kind widens an optional
map, and a body written before this change simply has no collection of that kind, which is
not an error (registry C-006). No persisted data exists in the new shape and none changes
shape, so there is no migration to write.

What a schedule record holds, which record it points at, and which of the four kinds may be
scheduled are the `review-schedule` contract's, not baseline's (DEC-005: each kind's fields
belong to its module's contract).

## Alternatives considered
### The tempo, the due date, and the last-reviewed date as fields on registry's hazard, control, and platform records, and on the reference entry
Rejected because: it puts one slice's data inside two other modules' record kinds, and
neither way out of that is available. Either `review-schedule` reads and writes
`registry`'s and `reference-register`'s collections — a record module depending on another
record module, which DEC-004 decomposed the modules specifically to avoid, and which would
also need those modules' malformed-record rules restated in a third place — or the
operations move into the owning modules, so that setting a tempo and completing a review
become `registry` operations. The second contradicts SL-06 criterion 3, which names
`review-schedule` as the module that exposes the completion operation, and it would leave
`review-schedule` owning nothing at all.

It is also not a backward-compatible change to the stored data, which the field reading is
usually chosen to be. Under registry C-008 an entry is malformed when "one of the kind's own
fields is missing or of the wrong shape", and every operation that reads the collection then
rejects with that kind's `Malformed*Error` and returns nothing. A tempo field added to
`hazard` would make every hazard, control, and platform already written malformed, so every
read of those collections would refuse until a migration rewrote the folder — a schema
version bump and a migration, against this decision's none of either.

## Consequences
- Makes easy: the SL-06 CONTRACT session, which now has a kind of its own to own, with a
  header, a status, and a change-log history like any other record; adding a fifth
  reviewable kind later without touching what any other module stores; the overdue flag
  read from one record rather than assembled from four kinds' fields.
- Makes hard: any list that shows a hazard with its overdue flag, which is now a
  composition of `registry` and `review-schedule` done in `views` or `workflows` and never
  by one read (the cost DEC-004 already accepted for REQ-026 and REQ-068); and the schedule
  of a retired or deleted record, which the owning module's operation does not reach — what
  happens to it is the `review-schedule` contract's to say.
- Locks in: `review-schedule` in the kind list the change log names (REQ-045); a UUID as
  the schedule's identity, so a schedule is addressable in its own right and not only
  through the record it schedules.

## Reversal
Cost to undo: hours. No `review-schedule` contract, implementation, or stored record exists
yet, so undoing it is deleting two lines and a typedef from `baseline/types.js`, their tests,
and the CHG-002 row. The cost rises to days as soon as the contract and its suite exist, and
to a migration once a folder holds a schedule.
Would be triggered by: the SL-06 CONTRACT session finding that criterion 2's due date or
criterion 4's overdue flag cannot be stated without `review-schedule` reading a record
module's collection — which is a STOP and a re-examination of DEC-004's decomposition, not a
quiet move of the fields; or a later slice finding that every list of every kind needs its
schedules, so that the composition is paid everywhere and the edge saved nowhere.
