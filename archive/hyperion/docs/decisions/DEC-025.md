# DEC-025: A workflow is a stored record from its first step, the module that guides it makes the acts its steps name, and completion seals the record
Date: 2026-09-16
Status: accepted
Tier: interface

## Context
SL-07 is the slice for the five guided workflows and their immutable records. `workflows` is one
of the two composing modules DEC-004 placed above the record modules, and its CONTRACT session
cannot be written without three questions being answered together, because each answer decides
what the other two can be.

Where the acts a step makes are made: in `workflows`, or in `views` with `workflows` told
afterwards. When a workflow record exists: from its first step, or only once the workflow
completes. And what "once complete, no field of that record can be changed" (criterion 4,
REQ-039) is a refusal by — a check in every operation, or the absence of any operation that
would do it.

The three are coupled. If `views` makes the acts, `workflows` records steps it did not cause and
REQ-077's gate is not enforceable from here. If the record exists only at completion, there is no
in-progress state for REQ-039 to protect and REQ-049's "the workflows that user has in progress"
has nothing to read. And if the record exists from the first step, every step is a change to
stored data, which HZ-005 and REQ-045 say must carry an entry — so the cost of storing early is
paid in the change log and has to be accepted or the decision changed.

## Decision
**`workflows` makes the acts its steps name, and records the step in the same call.** Each step's
operation calls `registry` or `review-schedule` through its contract and appends the entry for
that step to the workflow record before it resolves. A step the record shows is an act that
happened, and an act this module makes is never made with no step to show it. This is DEC-016's
argument one layer up: a consumer that made the act and then told the workflow could drop the
second call, the workflow record would be missing a step it in fact took, and no test of either
module would see the hole — it is in neither diff.

**A workflow is a stored record from `startWorkflow`.** It is in `collections['workflow-record']`
with `state: 'in-progress'` and its `currentStep`, it changes as the user works, and it is saved
with the working body like any other record. Every one of those changes calls
`change-log.recordChange`, so a step that makes a `registry` act appends two entries — the
registry act's and the workflow record's — and a step that only records a decision appends one.

**Completion seals the record by there being no operation that would unseal it.** Every changing
operation of the contract refuses a record whose `state` is `'complete'`, and — the part that
does the work — the contract exposes no operation that edits a field of a workflow record at all,
complete or not. `entries` is only ever appended to, and `collections['workflow-record']` is
written by this module alone. REQ-039 is therefore kept by there being no call that would break
it rather than by a check anyone has to remember (P2).

## Alternatives considered
### `views` makes the acts and calls `workflows` to record each step
Rejected because: the hole is in neither diff. A `views` that made the `registry` call and
omitted the `workflows` call would produce a completed workflow record that does not name an act
it caused, and a `workflows` that was never called cannot know. It is exactly the shape DEC-016
rejected for `registry` and `change-log`, and REQ-077 makes it worse here: the gate that requires
each residual to be settled before the control comes off can only be enforced by the module that
makes the exclusion, so putting the exclusion in `views` would leave REQ-077 with no module able
to refuse.

### `workflows` is a pure state machine and exposes no acts at all
Rejected because: it is the previous alternative with the coupling made explicit, and it leaves
the five workflows as five sequences of screen state with the composition — `listPlatformHazards`
then `completeReview` per record, or `setRating` then `excludeControlFromPlatform` per hazard — in
`views`. DEC-004 put the composing modules here precisely so that an operation spanning record
modules is not composed in the screen. It would also make criterion 3's and criterion 5's clauses
`views`', where they would sit beside seventeen screen kinds rather than beside the record that
has to show what was done.

### `workflows` re-exposes the registry acts as its own operations, so a caller may use either
Rejected because: it is a second surface for one act and a stop condition ("you are about to
write a second implementation of something that already exists"). The operations here are steps —
each one is a submission at a named step of a running workflow, refused when the workflow is not
at that step — and not a second way to create a hazard. A caller that wants to create a hazard
outside a workflow calls `registry`, which `views` already does.

### The workflow record is written only at completion; steps are held in the screen
Rejected because: REQ-039 would have nothing to refuse — a record that first exists complete is
immutable by having no earlier state — and REQ-049's in-progress list would be a property of one
open browser tab rather than of the saved data, so a user who closed Pivot would lose a
half-finished review with no record that it happened. It also loses the entries: what a user
decided at step two of a workflow they abandoned at step three is exactly what an abandoned
workflow should still say (C-012), and a screen that is gone cannot say it.

### The workflow record is stored but its in-progress changes are not logged
Rejected because: it is stored data changing with no entry, which is HZ-005's never-statement and
REQ-045's clause for the kind. The noise it would save is real — a four-step workflow appends
seven entries where a completion-only record would append one — but the entries are cheap, they
carry `madeForPlatformId` so they land in nobody's acknowledgement queue when the act was made
for the platform it is about (`change-log` C-010, DEC-026), and a log that omits one record kind
is a log a reviewer cannot trust for any of them.

### Steps as one `submitStep` taking a union, versus one operation per step
Taken, in its first form: one `submitStep` whose submission is told apart by its `step`, plus
`advanceStep` for the steps that take any number. Rejected alternative: one operation per step,
which is ten operations whose only difference is which `registry` call they make and which would
make "a submission belongs to the step the workflow is at" ten clauses rather than one (C-004).
The cost is that `submitStep`'s per-step conditions are in its clauses rather than one row each in
the error table, which section 4 carries by naming the step in the row.

## Consequences
- Makes easy: a workflow record that cannot claim an act that did not happen; REQ-077's gate,
  which is one refusal in the module that makes the exclusion; SL-11's in-progress list, which is
  a read of the saved body; showing what an abandoned workflow did before it was abandoned; a
  completed record that is immutable because nothing can write it.
- Makes hard: a quiet workflow. Every step is in the change log, and a long review appends an
  entry per hazard decided plus one per record whose review completes. Correcting a step, which
  is not offered — an entry is appended once, so a mistake is abandoned and started again.
  Running a step's act outside its workflow, which is `registry`'s surface and not this one.
- Locks in: `workflows` → `registry`, `review-schedule`, `change-log` as edges in the dependency
  graph; the workflow record as stored working state rather than screen state; `entries` as
  append-only; no edit operation on this surface at any version, since adding one would reopen
  REQ-039.

## Reversal
Cost to undo: hours while no implementation exists. Days once one does: moving the acts to `views`
rewrites every step clause and every conformance test, and moving the record to completion-only
changes the stored shape of a kind that would by then hold data, which is a baseline-shaped
migration under CORE-CHG-002 even though `RecordKind` itself does not change. The entries already
written would survive either move — their shape is `change-log`'s and does not depend on this.
Would be triggered by: a workflow long enough that its per-step entries make the history unusable,
which is DEC-003's reversal trigger arriving through the log (`change-log` section 6) and would
force either fewer entries or a filtered history; or a second consumer needing to run a workflow's
steps in an order or a shape this surface cannot express, which would put the sequence back in the
caller.
